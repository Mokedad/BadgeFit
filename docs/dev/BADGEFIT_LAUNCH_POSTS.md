## The idea: sell the life, keep the app a secret

Like army recruiting ads: nobody sells the rifle, they sell who you become. BadgeFit posts are about
the applicant: the 5 am alarm, the beep test that beat you last time, the day you pass, the uniform
you want. The app is never the star. It shows up at the very end as "the thing I use", the secret
the people who pass have and the others don't. An unknown brand wins by being something people
feel they found, not something sold to them.

Rules for every post:

- **The person first, the app last.** No app screens in the first 10 seconds.
- **Steady footage only.** Phone on a tripod or a wall, or a gimbal for moving shots. No handheld
  walking shots, no shaky zooms. If an AI tool makes the clip, ask for "locked-off, stable camera,
  slow push-in" and reject anything that wobbles.
- **One promise, never a guarantee.** "Train like the ones who pass", never "you will pass".
- **Never police-branded.** No badges, logos, uniforms or "official". Not affiliated with any
  police service. Film only yourself, or people who said yes.
- **Music from inside Instagram or TikTok** (their licensed sounds), never a song added outside.

## The format: chat, ad, comment

Every video is three parts, 20 to 35 seconds in all:

1. **The chat (5 to 8 s).** You to camera, phone propped up, natural light, like talking to a
   mate. One line that stops the scroll.
2. **The ad (10 to 20 s).** Cinematic cut: slow motion, early morning light, sweat, the line on
   the track, chalk, breath. Few words, on screen or as a voice-over. Music builds.
3. **The ask (2 to 3 s).** Back to you: **"Comment PCT and I'll send you the link."**
   On screen: *Comment PCT*.

## Which of the "42 ad formats" to use (owner's examples, 18 November 2026)

Best for BadgeFit, in this order (all talking-head first, steady camera, ending "Comment PCT"):

1. **Transformation** (with **Challenge hook**): "I'm going from level 6 to 7.5 on the beep test
   by 6 December. Day 1." A numbered series; every video moves the number. The strongest one.
2. **Tier list:** "Ranking the six PCT tests, easiest to hardest." People argue in the comments.
3. **Myth vs fact:** "Myth: run every day for the beep test. Fact: two hard sessions and easy runs."
4. **Warning / you can avoid:** "3 mistakes that fail people on the PCT" (running wide on the
   turns, no warm-up, peaking too early).
5. **iPhone notes:** a screen recording of a note, "Things I wish I knew before my PCT", typed out.
   Cheap and feels real.
6. **Problem vs solution:** "Stuck at level 6?" then the fix (intervals, turning on the line).
7. **Reddit style / Google search:** a real question applicants ask, answered in 20 seconds.
8. **Us vs them:** "Applicants who pass vs applicants who don't": habits, never mocking people.
9. **Testimonial / text message:** only once real testers say something, with their OK.

Do not use: breaking news, reviews or zero stars (there are none yet, and made-up ones are
deceptive), offer, bundle, low-stock alert (it's free, fake scarcity backfires), emergency,
side effect, we're sorry, and the cartoon ones (claymation, doodle, AI podcast).

## The presenter: a realistic AI person

The owner does not talk on camera, so the talking formats use a realistic AI presenter made in
an AI avatar tool (for example HeyGen, Captions or Arcads: pick an off-the-shelf avatar, paste the
script, export 9:16). The owner's own training clips stay the montage.

Rules, so it stays honest and allowed:

- **The AI person is a coach, not an applicant.** It never says "I failed", "I passed" or "I
  use this app". It says "Most applicants stall at level 6. Here's the fix." First-person stories
  are only the owner's own, as words on his footage.
- **Label it.** Switch on TikTok's "AI-generated content" label and Instagram's "AI info" label
  when posting; both apps ask for it on realistic AI people.
- **A stock avatar only.** Never a real person's face or voice, never anyone who looks like a
  police officer, no uniform.
- **No made-up reviews or results** from the AI person.
